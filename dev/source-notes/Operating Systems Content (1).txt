Goal/Assessment  
Goal 
Where are we going? What is expected? What will the student be able to do, know, and 
value at the end? 
The student will be able to describe various scheduling algorithms. 
Assessment 
How do we measure? How will the student be assessed in the lessons and at the end of 
the module? Use general terms. Present this to the student at the beginning of the 
module. 
A student should be able to: 
• Differentiate between long-term and short-term scheduling. 
• Differentiate between preemptive and non-preemptive scheduling. 
• Describe priority for short-term scheduling and long-term scheduling. 
• Schedule a set of batch processes using First-In First-Out (FIFO) also known as 
First-Come First-Serve (FCFS), Shortest Job First (SJF) also known as Shortest 
Job Next (SJN), Shortest Remaining Time (SRT). 
• Calculate estimated total CPU time using exponential averaging. 
• Calculate turnaround time. 
• Define starvation. 
• Schedule a set of interactive processes using Round-Robin scheduling, 
Multilevel scheduling, and Multilevel feedback algorithm. 
• Describe effects of time quantum and context switch time on performance such 
as response time or instructions per second. 
• Describe issues for scheduling of real-time processes including the time interval 
within each input must be processed. 
• Schedule real-time processes using Rate Monotonic (RM) scheduling and 
Earliest Deadline First (EDF) scheduling. 
• Describe the performance of real-time process scheduling such as feasible 
schedules and CPU utilization. 
• Describe combined scheduling approaches such as the 2-tier approach and the 
2-tier approach with floating priorities.

• Calculate speedup gain using Amdahl’s Law. 
Hook 
Pique the student’s interest using a case study, interesting story, experiment, or 
disaster. 
Question: What is the best way determine which process goes first? 
Lessons 
Divide the module into logical lessons (4-5 days). Create lessons, activities, 
experiments, homework and/or quizzes based on these grain size lessons. 
Lesson: Principles of scheduling 
Lesson: Scheduling of batch processes 
Lesson: Scheduling of interactive processes 
Lesson: Scheduling of real-time processes 
Lesson: Combined approaches 
Lesson: Amdahl’s Law 
Representative Topics 
• Long-term vs short-term scheduling, preemptive vs non-preemptive scheduling, 
priority for short-term scheduling, priority for long-term scheduling, batch process, 
First-In First-Out (FIFO) or First-Come First-Serve (FCFS), Shortest Job First 
(SJF) also known as Shortest Job Next (SJN), Shortest Remaining Time (SRT), 
Estimating total CPU (exponential averaging), turnaround time, starvation, 
scheduling of batch processes with FIFO, SJF, and SRT, interactive process, 
round-robin scheduling, time quantum, multilevel scheduling, multilevel feedback 
algorithm (different Q and change priority dynamically), performance, response 
time, effects of time quantum and context switch time, real-time process, period, 
rate monotonic (RM) scheduling, earliest deadline first (EDF) scheduling, 
performance, feasible schedule, CPU utilization (U), 2-tier approach, 2-tier 
approach with floating priorities, Amdahl’s Law
